# Helping someone find their member ID

## Where the number is
- **Medicare Advantage or a private plan** — the card comes from the insurer
  (UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Humana, Anthem, Blue Cross Blue Shield). Look for
  **Member ID**, **Member Number**, **Subscriber ID**, or **ID #**, usually on the
  front, upper area.
- **Original Medicare** — the card is **red, white and blue**. The number is labelled
  **Medicare Number**. It is 11 characters, a mix of numbers and capital letters,
  printed in groups of four (for example `1EG4-TE5-MK73`).
- Many plans also print the number in the member portal or app, and on the
  Explanation of Benefits letters that arrive by mail.

## Format is forgiving
Dashes and spaces do not matter. Letters can be typed in lower case. If a number
doesn't match, that is almost always a plan-formatting difference — not the patient's
mistake. Say so.

## If the card isn't nearby
Good options, all equal:
- Take a photo of the front and back later.
- Get a text or email link to finish whenever the card is handy.
- Let the Care Coordinator take care of it on the already-booked call.

Never make the patient feel they have to find it right now.

## Why we ask at all
Confirming the member ID lets us check that the patient's provider is in-network
before the call, spot any secondary coverage, and see the remaining deductible — which
can lower what they pay. It also means the coordinator call is about their care instead
of collecting numbers.

## What we do not know
We cannot tell from an eligibility check whether someone has **Medigap**. Supplemental
coverage has to be inferred or confirmed by a person. Never claim to know it.
