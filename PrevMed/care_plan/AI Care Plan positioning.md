# AI Care Plan Positioning — Top 3 Insights for the Landing Page

## Context

This document organizes the strongest patient insights from 70+ clinic intake calls into a marketing-facing framing for the AI Care Plan landing page. The audience is 65+ Medicare patients (and likely caregivers) considering Bold Care. Bold's core principle is **Confidence > Performance** — the product's job is to remove uncertainty, not to add metrics or competition. So the landing page should make people feel *seen, understood, and relieved* — not impressed by tech.

Three documents from the clinic-call insights point to the same emotional center: **patients are tired of vague advice, rushed doctors, and fragmented care, and they want a clear, personalized plan that actually fits *their* body and *their* life.** The AI Care Plan can credibly promise exactly that — but only if the framing leads with the patient's frustration, not with the technology.

A note on framing AI for this audience: insights show some skepticism toward medication-heavy, depersonalized care. "AI" as a hero word can feel cold or threatening. **Lead with the human outcome ("a plan built for you") and let "AI" appear as the *how*, not the *what*.** The AI Care Plan is the engine; the promise is clarity.

## The Top 3 Insights

### Insight 1 — "I'm tired of vague advice. I need a concrete plan that actually works for *my* body."

**The pain (verbatim):**
- Peggy: *"A concrete plan that says if you do this, you'll lose 10 pounds in 2 months, something like that."*
- Jessie: *"I just need to see how I need to be eating instead of what I'm eating."*
- Leanna: *"I'll start this like super low-carb thing, and then after maybe 5 days, I start just feeling really flat… I'm wondering how to do all this in a way that actually helps me lose weight and keep a good mood going."*
- Mike: *"I've probably lost 100 pounds 10 or 12 times in my life."* (Yo-yo cycle from generic advice.)
- Mary Edwards: *"I know it's the stuff that I'm eating… And I said I need to change. And I need support."*

**Why this is gold for an AI Care Plan landing page:**
Patients have *already* done the generic plans — keto, low-carb, "eat better, move more." They've failed not because they lacked discipline, but because the advice wasn't built for *their* labs, *their* meds, *their* menopause, *their* insulin sensitivity, *their* schedule. AI Care Plan's unique promise is synthesis: it can read the whole picture and produce a plan that's *specific* in a way a generic article or a 15-minute PCP visit can't be.

**Landing page framing direction:**
- Headline territory: "A plan built for *your* body — not everyone else's." / "Stop guessing. Start with a plan that's actually yours."
- Use the contrast: vague vs. specific. Generic ("eat fewer carbs") vs. theirs ("based on your A1c and your last lab, here's exactly what to eat this week").
- Anti-pattern to avoid: don't say "personalized" — *show* it. Show a plan that names their condition, their medication, their target.

---

### Insight 2 — "My doctor only has 15 minutes. Nobody is looking at the *whole* me."

**The pain (verbatim):**
- Catherine: *"With just my primary care and my cardiologist… you spend 5–10 minutes with them, and you're out the door."*
- Patty: *"He told me that he can only spend 15 minutes with me and I can only ask 3 questions… he fired me as his patient."*
- Bernice: *"I'm going to go beyond the doctor that only gives me 15 minutes for my appointment."*
- Dina: *"My doctor just doesn't have the time… it's still not the kind of appointment where I can sit down and talk to somebody."*
- Diana: *"If all these doctors came together to help me improve holistically, it would be absolutely amazing — that's healthcare to me."*
- Lorraine: wanted blood test reviewed *"nutritionally and hormonally and thyroid"* — together, not in silos.
- Romy: managing diabetes, lupus, BP, fatty liver, suspected thyroid — all separate, none connected.

**Why this is gold for an AI Care Plan landing page:**
This is the *deepest* emotional driver in the data. It's not just frustration with one doctor — it's a feeling of being **unseen as a whole person**. The AI Care Plan's structural advantage over a human-only model is exactly this: it can read across labs, medications, conditions, lifestyle, goals, and check-ins simultaneously, and present the synthesis to *both* the patient and the provider before the visit. The AI does the connecting-the-dots that a 15-minute visit can't.

This insight is also where AI earns its credibility: patients aren't asking for "AI." They're asking for **someone who finally sees the whole picture.** That's something AI can plausibly deliver in a way a rushed PCP cannot.

**Landing page framing direction:**
- Headline territory: "Finally — care that sees the whole you." / "Your labs, your meds, your goals — connected, not scattered."
- Position AI as the synthesis layer that *makes the human visit better*, not as a replacement for the human. ("Your provider walks in already knowing your story.")
- Show the contrast: the 15-minute visit vs. a plan informed by everything that matters.
- This also addresses the anti-medication subset (Diana, Bernice, Mary): "holistic" framing resonates with patients exhausted by being handed prescriptions instead of plans.

---

### Insight 3 — "I don't just need a plan. I need someone to keep me on track."

**The pain (verbatim):**
- Peggy: *"They'd call me and go, 'How you doing? All right, well, now I weigh this'… Is that what I could do on this program?"* — followed by *"Just like having a personal trainer. You don't really need the guy, but they make you go in there and do the work."*
- Lydia: *"I think it would be wonderful if I could have you guys there for me… it's not replacing, it's adding on to it."*
- Teravetta: *"Is there a limit to the number of appointments within a given year?"*
- Catherine: *"How often do you have the visits with the doctors like that?"*
- Becky: post-50-pound loss, stuck at a plateau — *needs the next chapter, not a restart*.
- Mike: 100 pounds lost 10–12 times — the missing piece is *sustained* support, not initial motivation.

**Why this is gold for an AI Care Plan landing page:**
Patients are explicit: a one-time plan isn't enough. They want **a plan that keeps showing up.** This is where AI Care Plan can credibly differentiate from "see a nutritionist once" or "download a PDF." The plan *adapts* — to new labs, to plateaus, to a rough week, to a new medication — and a real human checks in. AI handles the adaptation and pattern-spotting; the human handles the relationship.

This insight also reframes "AI" from a cold word into a warm one: AI is what makes it possible for the plan to *remember you, notice your patterns, and keep up.*

**Landing page framing direction:**
- Headline territory: "A plan that adapts as you do." / "Not a one-time visit. An ongoing partner."
- Make ongoing-ness concrete: unlimited visits, check-ins between visits, the plan updating when their labs change.
- Pair AI with the human explicitly: "AI keeps your plan current. A real provider keeps you on track."
- This also defuses the "AI feels cold" risk by anchoring AI in the *continuity* it enables, not in replacing the human.

## How the three insights stack into a landing page narrative

These three insights aren't independent — they form a story arc that maps to a hero + supporting sections:

1. **Hook (Insight 1):** *You've tried the generic plans. They didn't work because they weren't yours.*
2. **Differentiator (Insight 2):** *We see the whole you — labs, meds, conditions, goals — together. Your provider walks in already knowing your story.*
3. **Promise of staying power (Insight 3):** *Your plan adapts as you do. Real humans stay with you. Unlimited visits, ongoing check-ins.*

Three secondary themes from the data that can support — but probably shouldn't lead — the page:
- **Cost / Medicare coverage** ("$0 with most Medicare plans") — strong conversion driver, but not an emotional hook. Belongs near the CTA, not in the hero.
- **Aging independently** (Becky's 95-year-old mother, Bart wanting to travel) — emotionally powerful but more aligned with overall Bold positioning than AI Care Plan specifically.
- **Anti-medication / preventative** (Diana, Bernice) — strong segment but polarizing as a hero message; better as a supporting beat under Insight 2.

---

## What to *avoid* in the framing (also from the insights)

- **Don't lead with "AI."** Lead with the patient outcome. AI is the *how*, the trust-builder once they're already interested — not the headline.
- **Don't promise a "personalized plan"** without showing what makes it personal. The word is exhausted; the proof is in specifics (their condition, their lab, their medication).
- **Don't make it feel like a tech product.** This audience has presbyopia, arthritis, and decision fatigue. Calm, clear, human imagery; large type; no jargon.
- **Don't position AI as replacing the doctor.** Patients explicitly want human connection (Bernice: *"How somebody over Zoom is going to listen to you?"*). Position AI as what makes the human visit *better*.

## Verification — how to know the framing is working

Once draft copy exists, test against these checks (all derived from the source insights):

1. **The "Peggy test":** Does the page answer Peggy's literal question — *"A concrete plan that says if you do this, you'll lose 10 pounds in 2 months… is that what I could do on this program?"* — with a confident yes?
2. **The "Diana test":** Does the page convince Diana that finally *"all these doctors came together"* through her plan?
3. **The "Mike test":** Does the page convince someone who has lost 100 pounds 10 times that *this* time will be different — not because of willpower, but because the plan adapts and someone stays with them?
4. **The 72-year-old uncertainty bar:** After reading the hero, does a 72-year-old feel *more confident* about what to do next, or more uncertain?
5. **Read aloud at 8th-grade level.** No jargon. No "leverage," "optimize," "powered by." If it sounds like a tech pitch, rewrite.

Optional follow-up: once draft copy exists, run it past 2–3 of the actual interview patients (or proxies) and ask: *"Does this sound like it's for someone like you?"*
