**Working hypotheses for prototype targeting.**
Bold GLP-1 Companion Product · Q3 2026 launch
Based on the 2026-05-05 bullseye research readout (n = 4)
Last updated: 2026-05-20

> **Read this first.** All participants are **confirmed adults age 65+, living in the United States, and Medicare-eligible.** That is the audience frame for both personas. Within that frame, these are needs-based archetypes built from a 4-person sample — good enough to target prototypes; not demographically validated beyond the 65+ US Medicare-eligible cohort. The next research wave (larger GLP-1 survey + screener redesign) is the prerequisite to graduate them further. See "Honesty Section" at the end.

# Persona 1 — Tried-and-Burned

### Snapshot

Has direct GLP-1 experience that went poorly — bad side effects, weak weight-loss results, surprise costs, or all three. They walked away skeptical and are looking for a credibly *different* path back.

### In their own voice

> *"Last year I did GLP1 uh semicluide for like six weeks. It was the worst experience of my life. Not only that, I only lost seven pounds. Cost me $400 to lose seven pounds and I felt effects… If it's okay, if I could do a pill, that'd be great. Pills easy. Pen second. Compounded with a vial like that. No way. Definitely no."*
>
> — Participant, 2026-05-05 readout transcript, 00:12:29

### Dominant emotion

**Skepticism.** *"Why would this time be any different?"*

### Top fears

- Side effects (nausea, constipation, headaches) returning.
- Paying again and getting weak results — *"$400 to lose seven pounds."*
- Being put back on a shot, pen, or compounded vial.

### Top needs

- A credibly *different* path — pill-first form factor, not the one they failed on.
- Active side-effect management with a provider as **safety net**, not as a coach.
- Cost certainty up front — no surprise bills.
- Proof they're protecting muscle and bone, not just bouncing on the scale.

### What success looks like for them

They stay on the medication past the 6-week mark where they quit last time, because:

1. The side effects are actively managed.
2. They can see they're not just losing scale weight — they're protecting strength.
3. The cost is stable and explained.

### Implications for design

- **Lead with side-effect management and form-factor choice.** Don't bury either.
- **Never** show a vial, syringe, or compounded medication in hero imagery.
- **Pair every safety signal** (FDA, name brand) with a coverage/cost signal in the same view.
- **Body-composition proof has to feel low-effort.** Burdensome measurement collapses the value prop.
- **Acknowledge the prior experience.** A copy line like *"if a GLP-1 didn't work for you before, here's what we do differently"* will land harder than a generic welcome.

### Evidence

- Bad-side-effects + bad-results frame: 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:12:29.
- Form-factor preference (pill > pen >> injection >> vial): findings.md #2.
- $50/mo as compelling against the $300–$700 anchor users carry for GLP-1s: findings.md #19.
- Body composition as the differentiator over scale weight: findings.md #6.

# Persona 2 — Curious-but-Wary

### Snapshot

Has never tried a GLP-1. Has heard the success stories (a friend lost weight) and the horror stories (someone they know was sick for weeks). They're considering it for the first time, often as Medicare coverage opens up in July 2026.

### In their own voice

> *"I mean, much easier to do the pill for me because I'm definitely afraid of shots."*
>
> — Participant, 2026-05-05 readout transcript, 00:13:23

> *"As you get older you have to worry about bone density and stuff, but body composition — it stands out more than the rest of the other ones here."*
>
> — Participant, 2026-05-05 readout transcript, 00:15:42

### Dominant emotion

**Caution.** *"I want this to work, but I'm scared of what could happen to me."*

### Top fears

- Needles.
- The side-effect stories they've heard from friends and family.
- Cost, even with Medicare in the picture.
- *"Is this even safe for someone my age?"*

### Top needs

- Strong trust scaffolding up front: FDA approval, name-brand recognition, provider in the loop.
- A gentle entry — the pill option as the default visual frame.
- A clear, early cost story tied to Medicare coverage.
- Reassurance that aging is *protected for*, not ignored — bone density, strength, balance.

### What success looks like for them

They feel safe enough to start. The first 30 days don't deliver the horror stories they were told to expect. They have a real person they can call when something feels off — but not one who pesters them with daily check-ins.

### Implications for design

- **Onboarding must earn first trust.** FDA + Medicare coverage signals must be visible in the first screen or two.
- **Provider-as-safety-net** language, not provider-as-coach. *"A clinician will check in with you about side effects and dosage"* — not *"Chat with your coach today!"*
- **Pill as default visual frame.** Pen as a clearly-offered secondary. No needle imagery anywhere.
- **Acknowledge the stories without amplifying them.** *"You've probably heard some scary stories about GLP-1s. Here's what we do to keep you safe."*
- **Surface body-composition framing early** — many in this group are aging-aware and want to protect strength and bone, not just shrink.

### Evidence

- Fear of shots: 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:13:23.
- Body composition + bone density as aging concerns: 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:15:42; findings.md #8.
- Trust signals (FDA, name brands) trigger cost questions — pair them: findings.md #4, principles.md #5.
- Provider as safety net, not coach: findings.md #5, principles.md #6.

# Universal constants (true for both personas)

Every prototype must respect these regardless of which persona it targets.

| Constant | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Pill > pen >> injection >> compounded vial | findings.md #2 |
| Guidance, not tracking. Help *before* the decision, not after. | findings.md #9–11, principles.md #1–2 |
| Real person for accountability + side-effect management — **not** chat coaching | findings.md #5, principles.md #6 |
| FDA approval and name brands matter but trigger cost questions — pair them | findings.md #4, principles.md #5 |
| Body composition (lean mass, bone density, fat-vs-muscle) is the differentiator, not scale weight | findings.md #6, principles.md #3 |
| Older-adult-appropriate by default — seated exercise options; standing tolerance can be under 5 minutes | findings.md #15–16, principles.md #7 |
| No wearables, no extra devices | findings.md #12, principles.md #8 |
| $50/month tied to Medicare coverage, surfaced early | findings.md #19, principles.md #11 |

# Honesty section — what these personas do NOT cover

| Gap | Why it matters | When it gets resolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ages** are not disclosed for any participant | We can't tune age-specific copy or imagery with confidence | Larger survey + screener redesign |
| **Health diagnoses** (pre-diabetic / diabetic / post-menopausal) are not attributed per person | Some recurring themes (restrictive-diet fatigue, bone density worry) likely correlate with diagnoses we can't see | Screener redesign |
| **Household / eating patterns** (cooking, takeout, spouse roles) are real in the transcript but not tied to a persona | The "75% takeout, spouse stopped cooking" quote could belong to either archetype | Larger survey |
| **Validated proportions** of Tried-and-Burned vs Curious-but-Wary in the broader audience | Roughly 3:1 in the n=4 sample — not generalizable | Larger survey |

**Bottom line:** Use these personas to target prototype work. Do **not** use them to make brand or marketing-strategy decisions yet. Re-evaluate the moment larger research lands.
