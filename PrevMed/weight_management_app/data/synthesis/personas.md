# Personas — Working Hypotheses

Two needs-based archetypes derived from the bullseye research (n=4, 2026-05-05 readout). They are split by **relationship to GLP-1** — the one segmentation axis the research directly supports — not by what they're asking for, since the audience converges on the same asks ([[value-props]]).

**Audience frame (true for both personas):** Confirmed adults **age 65+, living in the United States, and Medicare-eligible**. Every design choice should assume this floor.

> ⚠️ **These are working hypotheses for prototype targeting, not validated personas.**
> n=4 is too thin for demographic confidence. The transcript explicitly notes *"three of the four had either... tried them for like a short period of time and then one hadn't but had experience with someone"* (2026-05-05 readout, 00:28:27) — that is the only segmentation axis the sample can defend. The planned **larger GLP-1 survey + screener redesign** is the prerequisite to graduate these archetypes ([[open-questions]] → "Audience and segmentation").

---

## Persona 1 — Tried-and-Burned

**Snapshot:** Has direct GLP-1 experience that went poorly — bad side effects, weak weight-loss results, surprise costs, or all three. They walked away skeptical and are looking for a *credibly different* path back.

**Dominant emotion:** Skepticism — *"Why would this time be any different?"*

**Anchor quote:**
> *"Last year I did GLP1 uh semicluide for like six weeks. It was the worst experience of my life. Not only that, I only lost seven pounds. Cost me $400 to lose seven pounds and I felt effects... If it's okay, if I could do a pill, that'd be great. Pills easy. Pen second. Compounded with a vial like that. No way. Definitely no."*
> *(2026-05-05 transcript, 00:12:29.)*

**Top fears**
- Side effects (nausea, constipation, headaches) returning. ([[findings]] #3)
- Paying again and getting weak results. ([[findings]] #19–20)
- Being put back on a shot or compounded vial. ([[findings]] #2)

**Top needs**
- A credible *different* path — pill-first form factor, not the one they failed on. ([[principles]] #4)
- Active side-effect management with a provider as safety net, not as a coach. ([[principles]] #6)
- Cost certainty up front, not a surprise bill. ([[principles]] #11)
- Proof they're protecting muscle and bone — not just bouncing on the scale. ([[principles]] #3)

**What success looks like to them**
They stay on the medication past the 6-week mark where they quit last time, because the side effects are managed *and* they can see they're not just losing scale weight.

**Design implications for prototypes**
- Lead with side-effect management and form-factor choice. Don't bury either.
- **Never** show a vial, syringe, or compounded medication in hero imagery. ([[findings]] anti-patterns)
- Pair every safety signal (FDA, name brand) with a coverage/cost signal in the same view. ([[principles]] #5)
- Body-composition proof has to feel low-effort — burdensome measurement collapses the value prop. ([[principles]] #12)

## Persona 2 — Curious-but-Wary

**Snapshot:** Has never tried a GLP-1. Has heard the success stories (a friend lost weight) and the horror stories (someone they know was sick for weeks). Considering it for the first time, often as Medicare coverage opens up.

**Dominant emotion:** Caution — *"I want this to work, but I'm scared of what could happen to me."*

**Anchor quote:**
> *"I mean, much easier to do the pill for me because I'm definitely afraid of shots."*
> *(2026-05-05 transcript, 00:13:23.)*

**Top fears**
- Needles. ([[findings]] #2)
- The side-effect stories they've heard from friends/family. ([[findings]] #3)
- Cost, even with Medicare in the picture. ([[findings]] #20)
- *"Is this even safe for someone my age?"*

**Top needs**
- Strong trust scaffolding up front: FDA approval, name-brand recognition, provider in the loop. ([[principles]] #5–6)
- A gentle entry — the pill option as the default visual frame. ([[principles]] #4)
- A clear, early cost story tied to Medicare coverage. ([[principles]] #11)
- Reassurance that aging is *protected for*, not ignored — bone density, strength, balance. ([[findings]] #6, #8)

**What success looks like to them**
They feel safe enough to start. The first 30 days don't deliver the horror stories they were told to expect — and they have a real person they can call when something feels off.

**Design implications for prototypes**
- Onboarding has to **earn first trust** — FDA + Medicare coverage signals must be visible in the first screen or two.
- Provider-as-safety-net language, not provider-as-coach. ([[principles]] #6)
- Pill as default visual frame; pen as a clearly-offered secondary; no needle imagery.
- Acknowledge the stories without amplifying them — "you've probably heard..." beats glossy denial.

## Universal constants (true for both personas)

Lifted from [[findings]] and [[principles]] so neither persona repeats them. Every prototype must respect these regardless of which persona it targets:

- Pill > pen >> injection >> compounded vial. ([[findings]] #2)
- Guidance, not tracking. Help **before** the decision, not logging after. ([[findings]] #9–11, [[principles]] #1–2)
- Want a real person for accountability and side-effect management — **not** chat coaching. ([[findings]] #5, [[principles]] #6)
- FDA approval and name brands matter, but always trigger cost questions. Pair them. ([[findings]] #4, [[principles]] #5)
- Body composition (lean mass, bone density, fat-vs-muscle) is the differentiator, not scale weight. ([[findings]] #6, [[principles]] #3)
- Older-adult-appropriate by default — seated exercise options; standing tolerance can be under 5 minutes. ([[findings]] #15–16, [[principles]] #7)
- No wearables, no extra devices. ([[findings]] #12, [[principles]] #8)
- $50/month is the price anchor — surface it early, tied to Medicare coverage. ([[findings]] #19, [[principles]] #11)

## What these personas do NOT cover (honesty section)

- **Ages.** The readout never discloses participant ages. "65+ Medicare-eligible" is the audience frame ([[decisions]] 2026-05-04), not a per-persona attribute.
- **Health diagnoses.** No participant-level pre-diabetic / diabetic / post-menopausal attribution. The transcript references restrictive-diet fatigue ([[findings]] #13) but does not tie it to a person.
- **Household and eating patterns.** The "75% takeout, spouse stopped cooking" quote (2026-05-05, 00:21:00) is real but not attributed to one of the two archetypes — it could belong to either.
- **Validated proportions.** We do not know what % of the broader audience is Tried-and-Burned vs Curious-but-Wary. The bullseye sample suggests roughly 3:1, but that is not generalizable.

These gaps are the work of the next research wave — see [[open-questions]] → "Audience and segmentation."
