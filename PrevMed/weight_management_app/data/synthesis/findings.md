# Findings — Validated User Insights

Validated insights from research with the Medicare-age GLP-1 target audience. Each finding cites its source so you can pull the original quote when needed.

**Method context:** Bullseye customer research sprint (4 qualitative interviews, target = enthusiastic wedge, not broad appeal). Sources: 2026-05-04 sync, 2026-05-05 readout (full transcript).

---

## GLP-1 form factor and trust

1. **GLP-1 form factor overrides every other product factor.** Whether the user gets a needle, pill, or compounded vial drives their decision before they evaluate the rest of the program. *(2026-05-05 readout, 00:10:14; 2026-05-04 sync.)*
2. **Hierarchy: pill > pen >> traditional injection >> compounded vial.** Quoted user: *"Pills easy. Pen second. Compounded with a vial like that. No way. Definitely no."* *(2026-05-05 transcript, 00:12:29.)*
3. **Cost and side effects are the two top fears**, competing for #1. Stories of nausea, constipation, headaches travel widely — most participants have heard them even if they haven't tried GLP-1s themselves. *(2026-05-05, 00:11:30; 00:28:27.)*
4. **FDA approval + name brands signal safety**, but mentioning brand names immediately triggers questions about cost and coverage. *(2026-05-05, 00:10:14.)*
5. **Provider oversight = trust signal**, specifically for managing side effects and dosage. Users do NOT want frequent provider conversations or generic check-ins. *(2026-05-05, 00:18:50.)*

## Body composition over scale weight

6. **Body composition is the strongest differentiator after GLP-1 trust.** Users want to see lean muscle mass, fat loss vs muscle loss, and bone density tracked — not just a scale number. *(2026-05-05, 00:13:23.)*
7. **Metabolic health labs don't resonate.** Users feel they already get this from their PCP and don't want it duplicated. Don't lead with a "metabolic health score." *(2026-05-05, 00:14:37.)*
8. **Bone density and osteoporosis came up unprompted.** Users link it directly to aging concerns. *(2026-05-05 readout slide "Value Prop 2"; user quote "as you get older you have to worry about bone density.")*

## Guidance over tracking

9. **Users want guidance, not tracking.** Strong distaste for any prototype that asks them to log meals, exercise, or habits. *(2026-05-04 sync; 2026-05-05, 00:16:42, 00:19:54.)*
10. **Help before the decision, not logging after it.** Quoted user: *"In advance of the meal is more helpful to have guidance rather than after the fact."* *(2026-05-05 transcript, 00:19:54.)*
11. **Photo-based food tracking is OK temporarily**, only as a starter to seed personalization — not as a forever interaction. *(2026-05-05, 00:17:45.)*
12. **No wearables, no extra devices.** Even step counters on screens signaled "you have to wear something" and were rejected. *(2026-05-05, 00:18:50.)*

## Diet and exercise preferences

13. **Restrictive diets cause fatigue.** Keto, low-sugar (esp. for pre-diabetic/diabetic users) — users are tired of restriction and want a non-restrictive frame. *(2026-05-05, 00:17:45.)*
14. **Convenience for eating out and prepared/delivery food matters.** Many users (esp. 70+) cook less; spouses have stopped cooking; food is takeout or prepared. They want help choosing well at the point of decision. *(2026-05-05, 00:16:42, 00:21:00.)*
15. **Exercise must look accessible.** Seated/chair options, balance-safe routines, joint-friendly. If a screen shows exercise that looks inaccessible, users say "this isn't for me" and disengage. *(2026-05-05, 00:17:45, 00:18:50.)*
16. **Standing tolerance is real.** Quoted user: *"Anything that requires me to be standing up for any length of time, that's a problem. I can't stand for more than maybe five or seven minutes."* *(2026-05-05 transcript, 00:21:00.)*

## Marketing message

17. **"Lose fat, not strength" is the winning core differentiator.** It connects GLP-1s, aging, muscle, body composition, safety, and exercise in one promise. *(2026-05-05, slide "Winning value props" #2.)*
18. **Don't promise % body weight loss.** That's how competing GLP-1 brands market; Bold's wedge is the *kind* of weight lost. *(2026-05-05, 00:24:50.)*

## Pricing

19. **$50/month is highly compelling** against the $300–700/month anchor users have for GLP-1s. Users self-reported a 9/10 willingness to pay at $50. *(2026-05-05, 00:27:23.)* Treat stated willingness-to-pay with a grain of salt — only purchase behavior is real.
20. **Cost has historically been the top blocker to GLP-1 access** for this audience. *(2026-05-05, 00:27:23.)*
21. **86% of Bold patients pay $0 out of pocket.** Use this as a standout proof point — pair with any "Covered by Medicare" claim. *(Source: 2026-05-20 positioning doc.)*

## Demand signal (quantitative)

22. **Weight Management is the #1 onboarding "Chief Complaint" by a wide margin.** 50% of free-response answers (486 of 965) categorize to Weight Management. Distant runners-up: Energy & Fatigue 12% (113), Exercise/Mobility/Strength/Balance 6% (61), Chronic Pain 6% (55), Chronic & Metabolic non-pain 4% (41). Categorized by Gemini — may have miscategorizations, but the gap is large enough to be load-bearing. *(Source: 2026-05-20 positioning doc.)*
23. **Energy and pain are typically *contributing factors* to weight gain, not competing leads.** Sample user quotes: *"Being tired and lack of energy while trying to lose weight,"* *"Weight loss so I can get rid of joint pain,"* *"To lose weight for my lower back pain and my stomach and pain in my hips."* Use them as secondary messaging that reinforces the weight headline, not as separate value props. *(Source: 2026-05-20 positioning doc.)*

## Marketing reach (operational)

24. **Weight Management ads are consistently the top-performing Clinic top-of-funnel assets.** This is why the team is leaning into a weight-led positioning even before GLP-1s ship. *(Source: 2026-05-20 positioning doc.)*

## Marketing copy and UI (from Dovetail synthesis)

25. **"Strength, not restriction" is the single most resonant headline across the entire bullseye sample — 4/4 reacted positively, regardless of which concept they ultimately preferred.** It reframes the goal around gain, not deprivation. Should anchor the core value prop *across all concepts*, not be siloed in Prototype 1. Quoted users: *"Like what it says, it says about strength, not restriction. That's what I'm talking about."* (Jennifer), *"It's about strength, not restriction... there's a lot of foods that I love, and if I have a restriction…"* (Ron). *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insight #1.)*
26. **"Check My Coverage" is a trust anchor AND a conversion trigger — 4/4 reacted, 2 said it could drive plan-switching.** For Medicare Advantage users, the visible coverage check transformed reaction from skeptical to enthusiastic. *"It was approved by the FDA and my insurance probably cover it. I'm like, woo-hoo."* (Jennifer); *"Now also, all this one and the one before, it says check my coverage. That's important."* (Ron). Should appear early and prominently — not buried at the bottom — and may warrant being interactive rather than a passive CTA. Time-sensitive given the July 2026 Medicare GLP-1 subsidy. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insight #3.)*
27. **Provider supervision is *motivational accountability*, not just clinical safety.** 3/4 (Jennifer, Ron, Linda) called this out as a primary motivator. Sharpens [[principles]] #6 and finding #5. Jennifer: *"Because I'm not in it by myself... I don't feel so well, maybe I should just quit. Whereas the doctor can be alerted to that and do something maybe different."* Lead with the *outcome* ("someone who keeps you on track / notices / adjusts"), not the *credential* ("doctor-supervised"). A nutritionist or coach role — not strictly a physician — may be a meaningful differentiator. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insights #4, #10.)*
28. **Lean mass tracking triggers a universal "how does that work?" follow-up.** All three participants who found it interesting (Jennifer, Robin, Ron) immediately asked how it would be measured ("do I need a blood draw / device / lab?"). The feature is compelling but the prototype does not adequately explain the measurement method. Removing the "black box" is a likely conversion lever. Reinforces [[open-questions]] "measurement strategy." *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insight #5.)*
29. **Microdosing / taper resonates as a *GLP-1 entry philosophy*, not just a Concept 3 feature.** Only 1/4 (Robin) had strong unprompted enthusiasm, but the underlying logic — fewer side effects, easier tapering, less drug dependency — speaks to general GLP-1 long-term-use anxiety across the sample. Worth testing as a cross-concept framing to reduce hesitancy earlier in the decision journey. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insight #7.)*
30. **Concept 2's headline "Lose weight by going beyond the scale" is too generic** for what the concept actually offers (labs, hormone balance, insulin sensitivity, cortisol). Robin flagged this directly. If we keep the lab-depth angle anywhere in product or marketing, the headline must communicate clinical depth more concretely. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, insight #6.)*
31. **Composite ideal program — what participants designed for themselves when combining concepts:** Concept 1's lean-mass tracking + "strength, not restriction" messaging + Concept 2's lab-based clinical depth + FDA-only GLP-1 options + microdose/taper philosophy + a human coach or nutritionist for accountability. Use this as a north-star spec when scoping the app — it is the union, not the intersection, of what tested well. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail bullseye report, "Concept Preference Summary.")*
32. **Jennifer's lived prior GLP-1 experience is a load-bearing user story.** Tried semaglutide injections, paid $400/month out of pocket, lost only 7 lbs, stopped due to nausea, constipation, and irritability. She now prefers a pill. This is the single richest first-person account in the sample for "previously tried, churned, would re-engage if it felt safer/cheaper." Worth quoting in onboarding social proof or value-prop pages. *(Source: 2026-05-05 Dovetail insight doc, Value Prop 1 evidence; 2026-05-05 readout.)*

## Landing-page craft (from 2026-05-21 LP review)

33. **Appointment length is a tangible differentiator and should carry a number.** A specific minute count makes "long appointments" credible in a way that abstract claims don't. Pair with "doctors and NPs" wording for an expertise signal where the regulatory frame allows it. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:04:07.)*
34. **Accountability beats "care plan" as the load-bearing framing for follow-ups.** "Held accountable for real change" lands as a benefit; "follow-up appointments and a care plan" reads as descriptive table stakes. The reason follow-ups matter is the accountability they create — say *that*, not the mechanic. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:05:16.)*
35. **"Personalized" + "practical" + "doable" are the resonant care-plan modifiers.** "Provider-created" reads as table stakes ("of course it's from the doctor"); "personalized" / "personalized plan" / "personalized treatment plan" tested better in Tzu-Yi's research. "Doable" comes from the fitness side of the house — addresses the fear that what's being prescribed is something the user can't actually do. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:07:33–00:08:48.)*
36. **"Mindful" reads as fluffy** and should be cut from value-prop copy. Both Miranda and Eliza flagged it independently. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:09:53.)*
37. **Curate press logos hard: NYT in, B2B outlets (Med City News, TechCrunch) out.** Consumer credibility comes from consumer-recognizable outlets. B2B logos read as noise to a 65+ consumer audience and dilute the one strong signal (NYT). One anchor logo + clean treatment beats a logo wall. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:13:40.)*
38. **Member-count proof outperforms a logo wall.** "Thousands" reads as suspiciously low and should not be used. Use a concrete consumer number ("over 100,000," "hundreds of thousands") or app-store-style ratings. Trustpilot from the fitness side is on the table. The "12M eligible" fitness figure is *eligibility*, not members — don't conflate. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:14:37.)*
39. **Say "doctor" wherever the regulatory frame allows.** Most surfaces are restricted to "provider"; having "doctor" anywhere it's permissible is a credibility lever the LP currently leaves on the table. Use "Dr." / "NP" prefixes on provider cards to disambiguate years-of-experience numbers from age (a 17-year-veteran provider can read as a 17-year-old without the prefix). *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:11:24–00:12:24.)*
40. **Copy-only AB tests of LP changes risk an undetectable effect.** When the weight-led variant is structurally identical to the control — same modules, same order, same imagery — the differential is too small to read in a finite-sample AB test (Chris's direct concern). The fix is to ship copy + design changes together: surface nutrition/care-plan imagery above the fold, or swap the carousel module for a "pillars of a weight-loss plan" layout (Nutrition / Exercise / Habits / Older-adult strength). Alternative lightweight module: a standout quote from Deep about Bold's approach to weight loss. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:17:06–00:20:39.)*
41. **"Healthy body composition" message is under-represented in the current LP.** Belly fat / stubborn fat / not losing strength / bone density / osteoporosis are research-validated hooks ([[findings]] #6–8, #17) — they exist in the program story but don't surface above the fold in the current draft. Chris's read: this is the most under-conveyed of the six winning value props. *(Source: 2026-05-21 LP review, 00:22:03–00:24:54.)*

## What to avoid (anti-patterns)

- Vial/needle as the hero image. *(2026-05-05 slide "Value Prop 1, what to avoid.")*
- Leading with compounded medication.
- "GLP-1 access" framing without immediately clarifying cost, coverage, safety, oversight.
- Treating "FDA-approved" as a pure trust signal — it also triggers cost/coverage anxiety.
- Generic habit tracking as a primary feature.
- Step counters, sleep trackers, or anything implying a wearable is required.
- Generic-looking exercise imagery that doesn't read as older-adult-appropriate.
- Overstating coaching/oversight to the point it feels like surveillance.
- Asking users to manually log food or exercise.
