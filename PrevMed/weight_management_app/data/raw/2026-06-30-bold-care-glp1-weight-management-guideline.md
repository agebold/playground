## 1. Purpose

Defines Bold Care's evidence-based approach to initiating and managing GLP-1 receptor agonists and other AOMs for weight management in adults 65 and older. The program is **medically supervised, high-touch**, and integrates pharmacotherapy with comprehensive lifestyle medicine, nutrition counseling, personalized strength programming via Bold Fitness, and behavioral support.

Weight management in older adults improves outcomes in hypertension, Type 2 DM, dyslipidemia, CVD, osteoarthritis, and mental health. This guideline ensures safe, age-appropriate prescribing while preventing complications in the elderly (sarcopenia, dehydration, falls, loss of bone density, medication interactions).

## 2. Approved Indications

### 2.1. Weight Management (via GLP-1 Bridge — flat $50 copay)

Inclusion criteria — **18+** and one of:

1. **Obesity:** BMI ≥35
2. **Obesity:** BMI ≥30 AND one or more of: (A) heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), (B) uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >140 mm Hg or DBP >90 mm Hg despite concurrent treatment with two antihypertensives), or (C) chronic kidney disease stage 3a or above.
3. **Overweight:** BMI ≥27 AND (A) pre-diabetes (per ADA guidelines), (B) previous myocardial infarction, (C) previous stroke, or (D) symptomatic peripheral artery disease.

### 2.2. Weight Management (via Part D — variable cost to patient)

Medicare already covers GLP-1s for:

1. Type 2 Diabetes
2. Moderate to Severe Obstructive Sleep Apnea
3. MASH

If Weight Management patients have one of these comorbidities, we can prescribe GLP-1s following the guidelines below, **but this must be done via a standard Part D prescription (i.e., not the Medicare Bridge)** and the cost varies by patient. **Patients need to be aware of the cost prior to prescribing.**

For any patient not eligible for a GLP-1 via Medicare, we can 1) refer to PCP, or 2) offer lifestyle support/coaching.

### 2.3. Anthropometric Standards & Dosing Triage

Clinicians must use **both BMI and Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR)** to maximize accuracy and safeguard lean tissue.

- **Visceral Adiposity Threshold (WHtR >0.5):** indicates elevated central adiposity and high cardiometabolic risk. For borderline BMI (25–29.9), WHtR >0.5 serves as an approved weight-related comorbidity for GLP-1 authorization.
- **High Adiposity Titration Logic (WHtR ≥0.6 or BMI >35):** severe metabolic burden; triaged for steady, monthly up-titration to maximize weight loss, provided tolerability parameters are achieved.
- **Metabolic Responder Logic (WHtR <0.55 with BMI 25–30):** typically peripheral fat distribution / high metabolic responsiveness. Pause up-titration at intermediate maintenance tiers (e.g., Semaglutide 1.0 mg or Tirzepatide 5.0 mg) if metabolic/glycemic markers stabilize — protects lean skeletal muscle.
- **De-escalation Tie-In:** rapid BMI/WHtR reduction + signs of accelerating sarcopenia (failed functional metrics like the Chair Stand Test, or protein intake consistently <1.0 g/kg/day) ⇒ **mandatory down-titration** to the previous stable step, overriding any standard up-titration protocol.

## 3. Medicare and Older Adult (65+) Specific Clinical Considerations

Managing AOMs in a Medicare Advantage (MA) or traditional Medicare population requires an altered clinical lens focused on preserving functional capacity and preventing adverse drug events (ADEs).

- **Paradox of Sarcopenic Obesity:** excess adipose can mask a profound skeletal-muscle deficit. Inadvertent or rapid weight loss can strip 25–30% of total mass from lean tissue → frailty, fracture risk, reduced basal metabolic capacity.
- **Altered Pharmacokinetics:** age-related declines in eGFR and hepatic perfusion delay drug clearance, extending GLP-1 half-life and magnifying GI adverse profiles (prolonged nausea, vomiting, delayed gastric emptying).
- **Polypharmacy & Multimorbidity:** GLP-1 RAs dramatically modify the therapeutic requirements of concurrent agents (especially antihypertensives and secretagogue-class antidiabetics), demanding rapid, proactive dose reductions to avoid catastrophic orthostatic hypotension or severe hypoglycemia.
- **Value-Based Care Alignment:** success is measured not just by pounds lost but by **functional improvements** — reduced osteoarthritic knee/hip strain, enhanced cardiovascular endurance, safe preservation of glycemic metrics without severe hypoglycemia, and total prevention of fall incidents.
- **Functional Goals:** getting up and down from the floor more easily, walking farther, standing longer, balance confidence, carrying groceries, less pain, maintaining independence (ADLs/IADLs).
- **Establishment of Care Requirement:** Bold clinicians will **not prescribe or authorize any medication refills — including continuation of a patient's existing outside GLP-1 regimen — prior to completion of their first formal clinical visit with Bold.** A prescription can only be generated during or after the initial appointment once care is officially established and the clinician agrees to continuation.
- **Tracking Rule for Continued Therapy:** if a patient transferring to Bold wishes to continue an existing GLP-1 regimen, the clinical team must obtain and document their **initial, pre-medication BMI** (from a prior PCP note or medical record) to justify ongoing necessity and meet insurance compliance tracking standards.

## 4. Out of Scope / Exclusion Criteria

- BMI <25
- Patient without an active PCP
- **Patients over 80+** — *not a hard exclusion*, but clinicians should consider carefully whether medication is appropriate given limited research.

| Category | Exclusions |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Endocrine & Genetic Risks** | Type 1 diabetes mellitus (GLP-1s typically approved for Type 2); personal or family history of Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC); personal or family history of MEN 2 syndrome (Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2) |
| **2. Gastrointestinal & Pancreatic** | Active or recurrent pancreatitis; severe gastroparesis (delayed gastric emptying); chronic constipation; history of bowel obstruction |
| **3. Cardiovascular & Renal** | Recent cardiovascular event (<6 months); recent hospital stay for heart failure (<6 months) or uncontrolled heart failure; severe renal impairment (eGFR <15), caution with eGFR 15–30; end-stage renal disease / dialysis |
| **4. Psychological & Behavioral** | Active eating disorder (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder); severe, uncontrolled psychiatric illness that would preclude participation; active substance abuse |
| **5. Surgery & Oncology** | Bariatric surgery within the last 2 years; active cancer treatment (unless explicitly cleared by an oncologist) |
| **6. Pregnancy** | Pregnancy, nursing, or planning pregnancy within 2 months |

## 7. Comprehensive Dose Escalation and De-escalation Titration Grid

### Semaglutide (Wegovy / Ozempic) — SC injection

- **Up-titration:**
  1. 0.25 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  2. 0.5 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  3. 1.0 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  4. 1.7 mg weekly × 4 weeks (Wegovy only)
  5. Max: 2.4 mg weekly (Wegovy) / 2.0 mg weekly (Ozempic)
  - *Pause up-titration if WHtR <0.55 and weight loss is optimal.*
  - **Missed doses:** if 1 dose missed, keep same dosing; if 2 doses missed, down-titrate.
- **Down-titration trigger:** severe/intolerable nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea; eGFR decline >20%; rapid muscle volume loss.
- **Action:** immediately drop one dosing tier (e.g., 1.0 → 0.5 mg). Hold therapy for 1 week if acute dehydration or severe vomiting, then restart at the lower tier.
- **Essential watchouts:** Renal — dehydration driving AKI. Sarcopenia — lean mass depletion. GI — gallbladder disease, acute pancreatitis, profound anorexia. Cardiac — resting tachycardia.
- **Assessment / refill:** telehealth review at **Week 3 of every dosing cycle.** Clinician must review tolerability, BMI/WHtR velocity, and STEADI parameters before releasing the next dose-escalation prescription.

### Tirzepatide (Zepbound / Mounjaro) — SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 2.5 → 5.0 → 7.5 → 10.0 → 12.5 mg weekly (each × 4 weeks) → **Max 15.0 mg weekly.**
  - **Missed doses:** if 1 missed, keep same dosing; if 2 missed, down-titrate.
- **Down-titration trigger:** persistent grade 2/3 GI toxicity; rapid skeletal muscle wasting; inability to reach the 1.2 g/kg/day protein target.
- **Action:** de-escalate by 2.5 mg increments immediately. Maintain at the lower tolerated dose for a minimum of 8 weeks before attempting re-escalation.
- **Essential watchouts:** GI — pronounced delayed gastric emptying, intractable dyspepsia. Hypoglycemia — heightened risk if combined with basal insulins/SUs. Physical — orthostatic hypotension or dizzy spells.
- **Assessment / refill:** clinical check-in at **Day 21 of each monthly cycle.** Refill release requires **verification of daily protein logs and adherence to Bold Fitness tracking** before ordering the next dose increment.

### Liraglutide (Victoza) — SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 0.6 mg daily × 1 week → 1.2 mg daily × 1 week → **Max 1.8 mg daily.**
  - **Missed doses:** if 1 missed, keep same dosing; if 2 missed, down-titrate.
- **Down-titration trigger:** nausea resulting in a calorie deficit >50% of metabolic baseline; persistent daily abdominal cramping.
- **Action:** decrease by 0.6 mg increments (e.g., 1.8 → 1.2 mg, or 1.2 → 0.6 mg).
- **Essential watchouts:** Compliance — daily injection fatigue, pen-needle dexterity. Symptom profile — acute injection-site reactions, localized lipodystrophy, epigastric tenderness.
- **Assessment / refill:** telephone encounter at **Day 7 and Day 14.** Initial 30-day follow-up required; subsequent refills issued at 90-day intervals once a stable maintenance dose is established.

### Dulaglutide (Trulicity) — SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 0.75 → 1.5 → 3.0 mg weekly (each × 4 weeks) → **Max 4.5 mg weekly.**
  - **Missed doses:** if 1 missed, keep same dosing; if 2 missed, down-titrate.
- **Down-titration trigger:** recurrent bouts of diarrhea or unexpected abdominal distension; worsening renal metrics.
- **Action:** immediately return to the previous lower tier (e.g., 3.0 → 1.5 mg, or 1.5 → 0.75 mg).
- **Essential watchouts:** Device — single-dose auto-injector competency (visual or arthritic limitations). Systemic — persistent early satiety impacting essential micronutrient intake.
- **Assessment / refill:** clinical assessment via secure messaging or call at **Week 3 of each monthly step.** Approved monthly during escalation; extended to quarterly once a stable target tier is reached.

### Foundayo — oral medication

- **Titration schedule:**
  - Week 1–4: 0.8 mg once daily (starting dose)
  - Week 5–8: 2.5 mg once daily
  - Week 9–12: 5.5 mg once daily (minimum maintenance / step-up dose)
  - Week 13–16: 9 mg once daily
  - Week 17–20: 14.5 mg once daily
  - Week 21+: 17.2 mg once daily (maximum dosage)
- **Missed dose:** do not double the daily dose to catch up. If >7 consecutive days are missed, step down and reinitiate the escalation schedule at a lower dose.

## 8. Senior-Specific Safety Protocols

### 8.1. Sarcopenia Risk Mitigation
- **MANDATORY:** daily protein minimum **0.5 g/kg/day**; target **1.4–1.6 g/kg/day**
- **MANDATORY:** resistance training ≥2x/week via Bold Fitness
- **Monitor:** if weight loss >5%, assess lean mass (Chair Stand Test), height:waist ratio, calf circumference
- **Consider:** creatine supplementation (3–5 g/day) discussion; monitor for GI upset; refer to Fullscript or Thorne brand

### 8.2. Dehydration & AKI Prevention
- Counsel: minimum 6–8 glasses water/day
- Elderly thirst is blunted — schedule hydration reminders
- Check renal function at 4 weeks, 3 months, 6 months
- Severe GI symptoms (vomiting >2x/day): **HOLD dose, rehydrate, contact provider within 24h**

### 8.3. Falls & Dizziness Mitigation
- Rapid weight loss may exacerbate orthostatic hypotension
- Assess fall risk at every follow-up (STEADI)
- Adjust antihypertensives as weight decreases and BP improves
- Encourage Bold Fitness balance and strength classes

### 8.4. Gastroparesis & GI Management
- Start at LOWEST dose; extend titration intervals to 6–8 weeks if tolerating poorly
- Persistent nausea/vomiting: trial dose reduction BEFORE discontinuation
- Dietary: small, frequent meals; avoid high-fat foods
- PRN: Ondansetron 4–8 mg TID (though avoid if possible)

## 9. Concurrent Medication Adjustments

| Medication Class | Adjustment when starting GLP-1 | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sulfonylureas | Reduce dose by 50% at initiation | Hypoglycemia risk increases; falls risk in elderly |
| Insulin (basal/bolus) | Reduce basal by 10–20%; monitor glucose | Weight loss + GLP-1 → improved insulin sensitivity |
| Metformin | Continue; no adjustment needed | Complementary mechanisms; additive benefit |
| SGLT-2i | Continue; monitor volume status | Additive weight loss and CV benefit |
| Antihypertensives | Monitor BP; reduce if hypotension develops | Weight loss improves BP; over-treatment risk |
| Oral contraceptives | Recommend non-oral or backup method for 4 weeks after initiation **and after each dose escalation**; document counseling | GLP-1–mediated delayed gastric emptying reduces OC absorption and may reduce efficacy |

## 10. Clinical Escalation Pathway for Complex Cases

**Chain of command:** NP/PA → Collaborating MD → Medical Director → Chief Medical Officer → External Specialist

### 10.1. NP/PA → Collaborating MD (within 24 hours)
- **Renal impairment:** serum creatinine rises 30% or eGFR drops 20% from baseline
- **Severe intolerance:** persistent grade 2+ GI adverse events that don't resolve after a 1-step down-titration
- **Complex diabetes management:** intensive multi-dose (basal-bolus) insulin regimens, or erratic glucose with frequent mild hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL)
- **Poor efficacy:** weight plateau or zero reduction over a continuous 12-week span despite confirmed adherence and lifestyle alignment

### 10.2. MD → Medical Director / CMO
- **Complex diagnostic dilemmas:** suspected subclinical pancreatic inflammation or atypical gallbladder presentation requiring specialized imaging
- **Off-label combinations:** requests/rationale to combine a GLP-1 RA with secondary AOMs not authorized in standard guidelines (e.g., Contrave, or Metformin combined with max-dose GLP-1)
- **Severe behavioral adjustments:** new-onset or worsening depressive metrics or suicidal ideation on PHQ screens → prompt drug suspension and care coordination

### 10.3. Direct Referrals to External Specialty Care
- **Gastroenterology:** refractory gastroparesis, persistent intractable vomiting, or suspected biliary tract obstruction requiring advanced endoscopic evaluation
- **Endocrinology:** brittle Type 2 diabetes, recurrent severe hypoglycemia requiring external assistance, or concurrent complex endocrine neoplasias
- **Nephrology:** persistent, unresolving stage 3b/4 CKD (eGFR consistently <30 mL/min/1.73m²) or acute progressive kidney injury
- **Bariatric Surgery/Medicine:** non-responders with BMI ≥40 kg/m² or severe metabolic comorbidities who fail max-tolerated AOM therapy over 6 months

- Counsel: discontinue GLP-1 at least 2 months before planned conception

## 12. Side Effect Management

| Side Effect | Frequency | Management in 65+ | When to escalate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nausea | Very common (30–50%) | Smaller meals, avoid high-fat, ginger tea, ondansetron PRN; slower titration | Unable to eat/drink >24h → hold dose, hydrate, contact provider |
| Vomiting | Common (10–20%) | Same as nausea; emphasize hydration with electrolytes | Persistent >48h → evaluate dehydration/AKI |
| Diarrhea | Common (10–20%) | Hydration, BRAT diet, loperamide PRN | Severe/bloody → rule out infectious cause |
| Constipation | Common (10–20%) | Gradual fiber supplementation, hydration, MiraLAX PRN | Severe / no BM >3 days → exam for obstruction |
| Gallbladder symptoms | Increased risk | RUQ pain, nausea after meals → urgent ultrasound | Acute RUQ with fever → ER; cholecystectomy discussion |
| Hypoglycemia | Rare (unless on SU/insulin) | Rule of 15; glucagon script for high-risk patients | Severe (LOC, seizure) → 911; IV dextrose |
| **Suspected pancreatitis** | Rare | Hold medication, do not restart; obtain serum lipase/amylase; CT or US | Refer emergently to ER if elevated lipase/amylase or any concerning CT/US findings |

## 14. Treatment Discontinuation Criteria

- **14.1 Inadequate response:** <5% weight loss at 6 months despite adherence → reassess diagnosis, consider medication switch, add second agent, or specialist referral. At **12 months**, target ≥10% total body weight loss; if not achieved despite adherence and dose optimization → consider medication switch, combination therapy, or referral to bariatric medicine.
- **14.2 Intolerable side effects:** persistent despite dose reduction → discontinue; trial alternative AOM.
- **14.3 Non-adherence:** missed >50% of doses over 3 months → reassess motivation, identify barriers, consider deprescribing.
- **14.4 New contraindication:** acute pancreatitis, MTC, pregnancy → immediate discontinuation.
- **14.5 Patient discontinuation request:** provide taper discussion; counsel on rapid weight regain risk.
- **14.6 Patient losing too much muscle mass** (WHtR still >0.5 while losing >10% body weight): provide taper discussion; counsel on rapid weight regain risk. *(Source labels this "14.5" a second time.)*

- [ ] Obesity (baseline BMI ___ | baseline WHtR ___)

**Objective clinical justification & baseline data:** baseline labs checked — eGFR ___ (date ___), HbA1c ___% (date ___); functional/safety screens — STEADI fall-risk score ___ (Low / Mod / High); clinical rationale notes.
