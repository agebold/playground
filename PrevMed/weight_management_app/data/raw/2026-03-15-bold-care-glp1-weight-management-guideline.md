## 1. Purpose

Defines Bold Care's evidence-based approach to initiating and managing GLP-1 receptor agonists and other AOMs for weight management (and Type 2 diabetes) in adults 65+. The program is **medically supervised, high-touch**, and integrates pharmacotherapy with comprehensive lifestyle medicine, nutrition counseling, personalized strength programming via Bold Fitness, and behavioral support.

Weight management in older adults improves outcomes in hypertension, T2DM, dyslipidemia, CVD, osteoarthritis, and mental health. This guideline ensures safe, age-appropriate prescribing while preventing complications in elderly (sarcopenia, dehydration, falls, medication interactions).

## 2. Approved Indications

### 2.1. Weight Management
- **Obesity:** BMI ≥30
- **Overweight:** BMI 25–29.9 with at least one weight-related comorbidity (T2DM, CVD, HTN, dyslipidemia, OSA, MASLD, OA)

For Bridge (18+):
- Obesity: BMI ≥35
- Obesity: BMI ≥30 AND one or more of: (A) HFpEF, (B) uncontrolled HTN (SBP >140 / DBP >90 despite two antihypertensives), or (C) CKD stage 3a or above
- Overweight: BMI ≥27 AND (A) pre-diabetes (per ADA), (B) prior MI, (C) prior stroke, or (D) symptomatic peripheral artery disease

### 2.2. Type 2 Diabetes
GLP-1 RA as **second-line therapy** (after metformin and lifestyle) for A1c ≥7.0%. Dual benefit: glycemic control + weight loss in overweight/obese patients.

### 2.3. Anthropometric Standards & Dosing Triage
Clinicians must use **both BMI and Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR)**:

- **Visceral Adiposity Threshold (WHtR >0.5):** indicates elevated central adiposity and high cardiometabolic risk. For BMI 25–29.9, WHtR >0.5 serves as the qualifying weight-related comorbidity.
- **High Adiposity Titration Logic (WHtR ≥0.6 or BMI >35):** triaged for steady monthly up-titration to maximize weight loss, contingent on tolerability.
- **Metabolic Responder Logic (WHtR <0.55 with BMI 25–30):** pause up-titration at intermediate maintenance tiers (e.g., Semaglutide 1.0 mg, Tirzepatide 5.0 mg) if metabolic/glycemic markers stabilize. Protects lean muscle.
- **De-escalation Tie-In:** rapid BMI/WHtR reduction + signs of sarcopenia (failed Chair Stand Test, protein <1.0 g/kg/day) ⇒ **mandatory down-titration** to the previous stable step, overriding any standard up-titration.

## 3. Medicare and Older Adult (65+) Specific Clinical Considerations

- **Paradox of Sarcopenic Obesity:** excess adipose can mask profound skeletal muscle deficit. Rapid weight loss can strip 25–30% of total mass from lean tissue → frailty, fracture risk, reduced BMR.
- **Altered Pharmacokinetics:** age-related decline in eGFR and hepatic perfusion delays GLP-1 clearance → magnified GI adverse events (prolonged nausea, vomiting, delayed gastric emptying).
- **Polypharmacy & Multimorbidity:** GLP-1 RAs modify the needs of concurrent meds (especially antihypertensives, sulfonylurea-class antidiabetics). Pre-emptive dose reductions are required to avoid orthostatic hypotension and severe hypoglycemia.
- **Value-Based Care Alignment:** success is measured in **functional improvements**, not pounds lost — reduced osteoarthritic strain, cardiovascular endurance, safe glycemic preservation, zero fall incidents.
- **Functional Goals:** floor transfers, walking farther, standing longer, balance confidence, carrying groceries, less pain, maintained independence (ADLs/IADLs).

## 4. Out of Scope / Exclusion Criteria

- BMI <25
- Patient without active PCP
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus
- Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
- Personal/family history of MEN 2 syndrome
- Active or recurrent pancreatitis (absolute exclusion)
- Pregnancy, nursing, or planning pregnancy within 2 months
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR <15); caution with eGFR 15–30; ESRD/dialysis excluded
- Active cancer treatment (unless cleared by oncologist)
- Active eating disorder (anorexia, bulimia, BED)
- Recent cardiovascular event (<6 months)
- Severe gastroparesis
- Recent HF hospitalization (<6 months) or uncontrolled HF
- Severe uncontrolled psychiatric illness precluding participation
- Bariatric surgery within last 2 years
- Active substance abuse
  - Heavy alcohol (>7 drinks/wk) + pancreatitis risk → exclusion
  - 4–7 drinks/wk → counsel on pancreatitis risk

## 6. Approved Medications and Dosing

All dosing in 65+ follows **"start low, go slow"**.

### 6.1. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

| Medication | Indication | Starting Dose | Max Dose | 65+ Considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Semaglutide (Wegovy) | Weight mgmt | 0.25 mg SC weekly | 2.4 mg weekly | Slower titration in frail; assess tolerability q4 wks |
| Tirzepatide (Zepbound) | Weight mgmt | 2.5 mg SC weekly | 15 mg weekly | Monitor GI; protein counseling critical |
| (Foundayo) | — | — | — | — |
| Semaglutide (Ozempic) | Type 2 DM | 0.25 mg SC weekly | 2 mg weekly | Monitor dehydration/AKI; sarcopenia risk; protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day |
| Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) | Type 2 DM | 2.5 mg SC weekly | 15 mg weekly | Superior weight loss; higher initial GI side effects |
| Liraglutide (Victoza) | Type 2 DM | 0.6 mg SC daily | 1.8 mg daily | Daily injection; lower efficacy; good for daily routine preference |
| Dulaglutide (Trulicity) | Type 2 DM | 0.75 mg SC weekly | 4.5 mg weekly | Well-tolerated; auto-injector; great for injection-naive elderly |

### 6.2. Other Anti-Obesity Medications

| Medication | Indication | Dosing (65+) | Considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Metformin (off-label) | Overweight/obese with insulin resistance | 500 mg → titrate to 1500–2000 mg (ER) | eGFR ≥30; GI common; start lower |
| Contrave | BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with comorbidity | 1 tab daily → 2 tabs BID | Avoid if seizure hx; monitor BP |

**NOT prescribed:** Phentermine (controlled), Qsymia (controlled component), compounded GLP-1s.

## 7. Comprehensive Dose Escalation and De-escalation Titration Grid

### Semaglutide (Wegovy / Ozempic) SC injection

- **Up-titration:**
  1. 0.25 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  2. 0.5 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  3. 1.0 mg weekly × 4 weeks
  4. 1.7 mg weekly × 4 weeks (Wegovy only)
  5. Max: 2.4 mg weekly (Wegovy) / 2.0 mg weekly (Ozempic)
  - *Pause up-titration if WHtR <0.55 and weight loss is optimal.*
- **Down-titration trigger:** severe/intolerable nausea, vomiting, diarrhea; eGFR decline >20%; rapid muscle volume loss.
- **Action:** drop one tier (e.g., 1.0 → 0.5). Hold therapy 1 week if acute dehydration/severe vomiting, then restart at lower tier.
- **Watchouts:** AKI from dehydration; lean mass depletion; gallbladder disease, acute pancreatitis, profound anorexia; resting tachycardia.
- **Assessment / refill:** telehealth review at Week 3 of every dosing cycle. Clinician must review tolerability, BMI/WHtR velocity, STEADI before releasing next escalation Rx.

### Tirzepatide (Zepbound / Mounjaro) SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 2.5 → 5.0 → 7.5 → 10.0 → 12.5 → 15.0 mg weekly (each × 4 weeks).
- **Down-titration trigger:** persistent grade 2/3 GI toxicity; rapid skeletal muscle wasting; inability to reach 1.2 g/kg/day protein.
- **Action:** de-escalate by 2.5 mg increments. Maintain lower tolerated dose ≥8 weeks before re-escalation.
- **Watchouts:** pronounced delayed gastric emptying, intractable dyspepsia; hypoglycemia if combined with basal insulin/SUs; orthostatic hypotension.
- **Assessment / refill:** clinical check-in Day 21 of each monthly cycle. **Refill release requires verification of daily protein logs and Bold Fitness tracking adherence.**

### Liraglutide (Victoza) SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 0.6 → 1.2 → 1.8 mg daily (each × 1 week).
- **Down-titration trigger:** nausea causing >50% calorie deficit from baseline; persistent abdominal cramping.
- **Action:** decrease by 0.6 mg increments.
- **Watchouts:** daily injection fatigue, pen needle dexterity; injection-site reactions, lipodystrophy, epigastric tenderness.
- **Assessment / refill:** telephone encounter Day 7 and Day 14. 30-day initial follow-up; refills q90 days once stable.

### Dulaglutide (Trulicity) SC injection

- **Up-titration:** 0.75 → 1.5 → 3.0 → 4.5 mg weekly (each × 4 weeks).
- **Down-titration trigger:** recurrent diarrhea or abdominal distension; worsening renal metrics.
- **Action:** return to previous lower tier.
- **Watchouts:** auto-injector competency (visual/arthritic limitations); persistent early satiety affecting micronutrient intake.
- **Assessment / refill:** secure message or call at Week 3 of each monthly step. Monthly during escalation; quarterly once stable.

## 8. Senior-Specific Safety Protocols

### 8.1. Sarcopenia Risk Mitigation
- **MANDATORY:** daily protein ≥1.2 g/kg/day; target 1.4–1.6 g/kg/day
- **MANDATORY:** resistance training ≥2x/week via Bold Fitness
- Monitor: if weight loss >5%, assess lean mass (Chair Stand Test), height:waist ratio, calf circumference
- Consider creatine supplementation (3–5 g/day) discussion

### 8.2. Dehydration & AKI Prevention
- Counsel: ≥6–8 glasses water/day
- Elderly thirst is blunted — schedule hydration reminders
- Renal function checks at 4 weeks, 3 months, 6 months
- **Severe GI symptoms (vomiting >2x/day):** HOLD dose, rehydrate, contact provider within 24h

### 8.3. Falls & Dizziness Mitigation
- Rapid weight loss may exacerbate orthostatic hypotension
- STEADI fall risk assessment at every follow-up
- Adjust antihypertensives as weight decreases and BP improves
- Encourage Bold Fitness balance and strength classes

### 8.4. Gastroparesis & GI Management
- Start at LOWEST dose; extend titration intervals to 6–8 weeks if tolerating poorly
- Persistent nausea/vomiting → try dose reduction BEFORE discontinuation
- Dietary: small frequent meals, avoid high-fat foods
- PRN: Ondansetron 4–8 mg TID (avoid if possible)

## 9. Concurrent Medication Adjustments

| Medication Class | Adjustment when starting GLP-1 | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sulfonylureas | Reduce dose by 50% at initiation | Hypoglycemia risk; falls risk in elderly |
| Insulin (basal/bolus) | Reduce basal by 10–20%; monitor glucose | Weight loss + GLP-1 → improved insulin sensitivity |
| Metformin | Continue; no adjustment | Complementary mechanisms; additive benefit |
| SGLT-2i | Continue; monitor volume | Additive weight loss + CV benefit |
| Antihypertensives | Monitor BP; reduce if hypotension develops | Weight loss improves BP; over-treatment risk |
| Oral contraceptives | Non-oral or backup method × 4 weeks after initiation and after each dose escalation. **Document counseling.** | Delayed gastric emptying reduces OC absorption |

## 10. Clinical Escalation Pathway for Complex Cases

**Chain of command:** NP/PA → Collaborating MD → Medical Director → CMO → External Specialist

### 10.1. NP/PA → Collaborating MD (within 24h)
- **Renal impairment:** creatinine ↑30% or eGFR ↓20% from baseline
- **Severe intolerance:** persistent grade 2+ GI events that don't resolve after 1-step down-titration
- **Complex diabetes:** intensive multi-dose insulin regimens or erratic glucose with frequent hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL)
- **Poor efficacy:** weight plateau or zero loss over 12 weeks despite adherence

### 10.2. MD → Medical Director / CMO
- **Complex diagnostic dilemmas:** suspected subclinical pancreatic inflammation or atypical gallbladder presentation
- **Off-label combinations:** GLP-1 RA + secondary AOMs not in standard guidelines (e.g., Contrave or Metformin with max-dose GLP-1)
- **Severe behavioral adjustments:** new/worsening depressive metrics or suicidal ideation on PHQ → prompt drug suspension + care coordination

### 10.3. Direct Referrals to External Specialty Care
- **Gastroenterology:** refractory gastroparesis, intractable vomiting, suspected biliary obstruction
- **Endocrinology:** brittle T2DM, recurrent severe hypoglycemia, complex endocrine neoplasias
- **Nephrology:** persistent stage 3b/4 CKD (eGFR <30) or acute progressive kidney injury
- **Bariatric Surgery/Medicine:** non-responders with BMI ≥40 or severe metabolic comorbidities failing max-tolerated AOM ≥6 months

## 11. Pregnancy Protocol
- Screen at initiation: urine or serum HCG
- Screen at every refill or visit
- Counsel: discontinue GLP-1 ≥2 months before planned conception
- Document patient understanding and agreement

## 12. Side Effect Management

| Side Effect | Frequency | Management in 65+ | When to escalate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nausea | Very common (30–50%) | Smaller meals, avoid high-fat, ginger tea, ondansetron PRN; slower titration | Unable to eat/drink >24h → hold dose, hydrate, contact provider |
| Vomiting | Common (10–20%) | Same as nausea; emphasize hydration w/ electrolytes | Persistent >48h → evaluate dehydration/AKI |
| Diarrhea | Common (10–20%) | Hydration, BRAT diet, loperamide PRN | Severe/bloody → rule out infectious cause |
| Constipation | Common (10–20%) | Gradual fiber, hydration, MiraLAX PRN | Severe / no BM >3 days → exam for obstruction |
| Gallbladder symptoms | Increased risk | RUQ pain, nausea after meals → urgent ultrasound | Acute RUQ + fever → ER; cholecystectomy discussion |
| Hypoglycemia | Rare (unless on SU/insulin) | Rule of 15; glucagon script for high-risk | Severe (LOC, seizure) → 911, IV dextrose |

## 13. Follow-Up Schedule

| Timepoint | Visit Type / Duration | Key Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-enrollment (Phase 1)** | Intake forms | Enrollment questionnaire, PCP verification, lab requisition, food/activity log |
| **Initial visit (Phase 2)** | MD/NP comprehensive, 45–60 min | Full assessment, care plan, AOM education if appropriate, goal-setting, labs ordered |
| **RD visit (Phase 3)** | Dietitian consultation, 30–45 min | Nutrition plan, macro targets, meal planning, supplement recommendations |
| **2–4 weeks** | MD/NP follow-up, 20–30 min | Tolerability, side effects, early weight trend, Bold Fitness engagement |
| **8 weeks** | MD/NP follow-up, 20–30 min | Weight progress, functional assessment, medication titration decision |
| **3 months** | MD/NP + RD, 20–30 min each | A1c recheck (if DM), weight plateau, side effect management, care plan update |
| **6 months** | Comprehensive, 30–45 min | Full reassessment, sarcopenia screen, falls assessment, medication optimization |
| **Ongoing (stable)** | Every 4–8 weeks → q3 months | Weight tracking, side effects, medication refill, lifestyle reinforcement |

## 14. Treatment Discontinuation Criteria

1. **Inadequate response:** <5% weight loss at 6 months despite adherence → reassess diagnosis, switch medication, add second agent, or specialist referral.
2. **Intolerable side effects:** persistent despite dose reduction → discontinue; trial alternative AOM.
3. **Non-adherence:** missed >50% doses over 3 months → reassess motivation, identify barriers, consider deprescribing.
4. **New contraindication:** acute pancreatitis, MTC, pregnancy → immediate discontinuation.
5. **Patient request:** provide taper discussion; counsel on rapid weight regain risk.

## 15. PCP Notification Template (summary)

Bold Care transmits via secure clinical fax or integrated EHR routing **within 48 hours** of any clinical initiation or substantial modification.

Notification includes: patient identifiers; action type (initiation / escalation / de-escalation / discontinuation); medication name, regimen, indication; baseline BMI/WHtR/A1c; baseline labs (eGFR, HbA1c) with dates; STEADI score; clinical rationale.

**Coordination requests to PCP:**
1. Monitor BP, consider down-titrating concurrent antihypertensives if hypotension occurs.
2. If patient on sulfonylurea or basal insulin, Bold has recommended initial dose reduction; PCP to monitor glycemic trends.
3. Bold will track periodic eGFR and HbA1c; results sharable bidirectionally to avoid duplicate draws.
