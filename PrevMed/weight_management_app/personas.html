All participants are confirmed adults age 65+, living in the United States, and Medicare-eligible. That is the audience frame for both personas. Within it, these are needs-based archetypes built from a 4-person sample — good enough to target prototypes, not demographically validated beyond the 65+ US Medicare-eligible cohort. The next research wave — a larger GLP-1 survey plus screener redesign — is the prerequisite to graduate them further. The "Honesty" page at the end is non-negotiable reading.

Persona 01 Personas · Weight Management App
Persona 01 · Skeptic

Tried-and-Burned

Has direct GLP-1 experience that went poorly. Walked away skeptical. Looking for a credibly different path back.

01
"

Last year I did GLP1 uh semicluide for like six weeks. It was the worst experience of my life. Cost me $400 to lose seven pounds and I felt effects… If it's okay, if I could do a pill, that'd be great. Pills easy. Pen second. Compounded with a vial like that. No way. Definitely no.

Participant · 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:12:29
Dominant emotion
Skepticism. "Why would this time be any different?"
First-30-seconds bar
Show what is different — or they bounce.

Top fears

  • Side effects (nausea, constipation, headaches) returning.
  • Paying again and getting weak results — "$400 to lose seven pounds."
  • Being put back on a shot, pen, or compounded vial.

Top needs

  • A credibly different path — pill-first form factor.
  • Active side-effect management with a provider as safety net.
  • Cost certainty up front — no surprise bills.
  • Proof they're protecting muscle and bone, not just dropping scale weight.

What success looks like for them

They stay on the medication past the 6-week mark where they quit last time, because the side effects are actively managed, they can see they're protecting strength (not just scale weight), and the cost is stable and explained.

Implications for design

  1. Lead with side-effect management and form-factor choice. Don't bury either behind feature-led copy.
  2. Never show a vial, syringe, or compounded medication in hero imagery.
  3. Pair every safety signal (FDA, name brand) with a coverage/cost signal in the same view.
  4. Body-composition proof has to feel low-effort. A burdensome measurement strategy collapses the value prop.
  5. Acknowledge the prior experience. "If a GLP-1 didn't work for you before, here's what we do differently" will land harder than a generic welcome.
Evidence Bad-side-effects + bad-results frame: 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:12:29 · Form-factor preference (pill > pen >> injection >> vial): findings.md #2 · $50/mo against the $300–$700 anchor: findings.md #19 · Body composition as differentiator over scale weight: findings.md #6.
Persona 02 Personas · Weight Management App
Persona 02 · Cautious

Curious-but-Wary

Has never tried a GLP-1. Has heard both the success stories and the horror stories. Considering it for the first time as Medicare coverage opens.

02
"

I mean, much easier to do the pill for me because I'm definitely afraid of shots. … As you get older you have to worry about bone density and stuff, but body composition — it stands out more than the rest of the other ones here.

Participants · 2026-05-05 transcript, 00:13:23 & 00:15:42
Dominant emotion
Caution. "I want this to work — but is it safe for me?"
First-30-seconds bar
Earn first trust — FDA, Medicare, provider, pill.

Top fears

  • Needles.
  • The side-effect stories they've heard from friends and family.
  • Cost, even with Medicare in the picture.
  • "Is this even safe for someone my age?"

Top needs

  • Strong trust scaffolding up front: FDA approval, name brands, provider in the loop.
  • A gentle entry — the pill option as the default visual frame.
  • A clear, early cost story tied to Medicare coverage.
  • Reassurance that aging is protected for, not ignored — bone density, strength, balance.

What success looks like for them

They feel safe enough to start. The first 30 days don't deliver the horror stories they were told to expect. They have a real person they can call when something feels off — but not one who pesters them with daily check-ins.

Implications for design

  1. Onboarding must earn first trust. FDA + Medicare coverage signals visible in the first screen or two.
  2. Provider-as-safety-net language, not provider-as-coach. "A clinician will check in on side effects and dosage" — not "Chat with your coach today!"
  3. Pill as default visual frame. Pen as a clearly-offered secondary. No needle imagery anywhere.
  4. Acknowledge the stories without amplifying them. "You've probably heard scary stories about GLP-1s. Here's what we do to keep you safe."
  5. Surface body-composition framing early — many in this group are aging-aware and want to protect strength and bone, not just shrink.

Section 03 Personas · Weight Management App
Honesty section

What these personas do not cover.

The bullseye sample was deliberately small. Treat every gap below as research debt — work to do before the personas earn the right to anchor anything beyond prototypes.

Gap Why it matters When it gets resolved
Ages Ages are not disclosed for any participant. We can't tune age-specific copy or imagery with confidence. Larger survey + screener redesign
Health diagnoses Pre-diabetic / diabetic / post-menopausal status is not attributed per person. Some recurring themes (restrictive-diet fatigue, bone density worry) likely correlate with diagnoses we can't see. Screener redesign
Household & eating patterns Cooking, takeout, spouse roles are real in the transcript but not tied to a persona. The "75% takeout, spouse stopped cooking" quote could belong to either archetype. Larger survey
Validated proportions Roughly 3:1 Tried-and-Burned vs Curious-but-Wary in the n=4 sample. Not generalizable to the broader Medicare-age audience. Larger survey
Bottom line

Use these personas to target prototype work. Do not use them to make brand or marketing-strategy decisions yet. Re-evaluate the moment the larger research lands — and if it contradicts the bullseye sample, this document is the first thing to update.
