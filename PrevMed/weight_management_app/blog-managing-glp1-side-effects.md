# Feeling Your Best on Your Weight-Loss Medication

*A simple guide to side effects — from your Bold care team*

Starting a weight-loss medication (sometimes called a GLP-1) can change how you feel at first. Most of these changes are mild. Most settle down as your body gets used to the medication.

This guide walks you through what you might feel and what you can do about it. None of it is a surprise to us. We help people through this every day — and we're right here with you.

---

## Two things to remember first

**1. Side effects are common, and they're usually manageable.** Feeling a little off early on does not mean something is wrong. It often means your body is adjusting.

**2. When in doubt, call us.** That's what your care team is here for. Please don't change your dose or stop your medication on your own. If something needs to change, we'll decide that together.

---

## The most common feelings — and what helps

### Feeling queasy (nausea)

This is the most common side effect. Many people feel it, especially early on.

**What helps:**
- Eat smaller meals, more often.
- Skip greasy or fried foods.
- Try ginger tea.
- Sip water slowly through the day.

**Call us if** you can't keep food or drink down for a full day.

### Throwing up (vomiting)

Some people get an upset stomach that leads to vomiting. It usually eases with time.

**What helps:**
- Sip clear fluids slowly.
- Drinks with electrolytes can help you stay hydrated.

**Call us if** you throw up more than a couple of times a day, or it lasts more than a day. We want to make sure you don't get dehydrated.

### Loose stools (diarrhea)

Your stomach may feel unsettled as your body adjusts.

**What helps:**
- Drink plenty of fluids.
- Stick to gentle foods like rice, toast, and bananas.

**Call us if** it's severe, or if you see blood.

### Trouble going (constipation)

The medication can slow your digestion, so things may back up.

**What helps:**
- Add fiber slowly — too much at once can make it worse.
- Drink water through the day.
- Keep moving; a short walk helps.

We can also suggest a gentle over-the-counter option that's right for you. **Call us if** you haven't had a bowel movement in more than three days.

---

## Staying hydrated (extra important after 65)

As we get older, we don't always feel thirsty, even when our body needs water. That makes it easy to fall behind — especially if you've had an upset stomach.

**What helps:**
- Aim for about 6 to 8 glasses of water a day.
- Keep a glass or bottle where you can see it.
- Set a reminder if that helps you remember.

Staying hydrated protects your kidneys and helps you feel steady on your feet. **Call us if** you feel very thirsty, dizzy, or notice you're not going to the bathroom much.

---

## Protecting your strength

This program is about **strength, not restriction.** As you lose weight, we want to make sure you're losing fat — not the muscle that keeps you strong and independent.

Two simple habits make a big difference:

- **Eat protein at every meal.** Good choices include eggs, yogurt, fish, chicken, beans, or a protein shake.
- **Do your Bold strength routine about twice a week.** There are seated options and gentle modifications, so you can do it in a way that works for your body.

Keeping your muscle helps you stay steady, carry groceries, get up from a chair, and keep doing the things you love. If your routine ever feels too hard, tell us — we'll adjust it with you.

---

## Feeling dizzy or lightheaded

As you lose weight, your blood pressure can improve. That's good news — but it can sometimes leave you feeling lightheaded, especially when you stand up.

**What helps:**
- Stand up slowly. Take a moment before you start walking.
- Keep drinking water.

If you take medicine for blood pressure, please let us know how you're feeling. We may need to adjust it as your health improves. **Call us if** you feel dizzy often, so we can keep you safe from falls.

---

## Less common, but good to know

These happen less often. We're sharing them so you know what to watch for.

- **Belly pain after meals.** Pain in the upper-right side of your belly, especially after eating, is worth a call. Let us know.
- **Low blood sugar.** This mainly happens if you also take diabetes medicine. Signs include feeling shaky, sweaty, or confused. If this could apply to you, we'll talk about it and adjust your other medicines.
- **Severe, constant stomach pain.** This one is rare, but important. Strong stomach pain that won't go away — often spreading to your back, sometimes with vomiting — needs attention right away. Don't wait.

---

## When to call us — and when it's an emergency

Keep this handy. It takes the guesswork out of what to do.

**Call your care team if you have:**
- Nausea or vomiting that keeps you from eating or drinking for a day
- Throwing up more than twice a day
- Diarrhea that's severe or has blood in it
- No bowel movement for more than three days
- Dizziness that keeps happening
- Belly pain after meals

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room if you have:**
- Severe, constant stomach pain, especially if it spreads to your back
- Belly pain with a fever
- Fainting, a seizure, or someone can't wake you
- Signs of serious dehydration you can't manage at home

When something doesn't feel right, you don't have to figure it out alone. Reach out — we'd always rather hear from you.

---

## The bottom line

Most side effects are mild and ease up as your body adjusts. The steps in this guide — smaller meals, plenty of water, protein, gentle movement, and standing up slowly — handle the great majority of them.

And you're not doing this by yourself. Your Bold provider is watching the full picture: your medication, your other health needs, and how you're feeling. Your follow-up visits are part of the plan, so we can adjust as you go.

Our goal is simple: help you feel your best and stay strong. Strength, not restriction — every step of the way.
