Your Eating Out Protein Checklist

A quick reference for restaurants, takeout, and prepared meals.

The protein scan: Look at any menu and find the protein first, then build your meal around it.

1
Restaurant ordering
  • Start with a protein: grilled chicken or fish entrée, shrimp cocktail, Greek salad with chicken, egg-based breakfast, or soup with beans/lentils.
  • Easy upgrades: side of cottage cheese instead of fries; add grilled chicken to any salad; order salmon instead of pasta.
  • Watch out for breaded/fried proteins (high saturated fat and sodium) and cream-based sauces (calories without much protein).
  • Mostly bread, pasta, or rice on the plate? Add a protein — side of grilled chicken or a cup of lentil soup.

3
Takeout & prepared meals
  • Make sure there's at least one dedicated protein source on the plate. If not, add one — most places will add grilled chicken or shrimp for a few dollars.
  • For meal kits: look for at least 25g protein per serving, clear nutrition labels, and dietary restriction options.
  • For grocery store "heat and eat" meals: check protein per serving, watch sodium, make sure ingredients are recognizable.
  • Add a glass of milk (dairy, lactose-free, or soy) to any takeout or prepared meal for an easy protein and calcium boost.

Rule of thumb: If you can't spot the protein on the plate, add one. These are tools, not rules — food should bring joy. On special occasions, enjoy the pasta and the bread basket; no guilt required.

The Protein Pantry Checklist

Stock these items and you'll never be more than a few minutes from a protein-rich meal.

  • Canned salmon

  • Greek yogurt
  • Cottage cheese
  • String cheese
  • Hard-boiled eggs (homemade or pre-made)
  • Deli turkey or chicken
  • Hummus
  • Milk
3
Freezer · minimal prep
  • Frozen salmon filletsBake from frozen in 15 min or thaw overnight.
  • Frozen shrimpThaws in 5 min, cooks in 3–4.
  • Frozen edamameMicrowave, done in 3 min.
  • Frozen chicken breasts or boneless, skinless thighs
  • Frozen bean burgers
4
Quick pairing ideas
  • Canned tuna or salmon + whole grain crackers = 20–25g protein in under 5 minutes.
  • Greek yogurt + handful of nuts = 24–26g protein, zero cooking.
  • Frozen shrimp + pre-cut veggies + teriyaki sauce = 22g protein in under 15 minutes.
  • Canned beans + shredded cheese + salsa + rice + Greek yogurt = 20g Tex-Mex bowl.
  • Hard-boiled eggs + string cheese + almonds = 19g protein snack, no prep.
  • Frozen salmon fillet + frozen stir-fry veggies + microwave rice = 28g hands-off dinner.
Tip
If you stock even half of this list, you'll always have a protein-rich option within arm's reach. Daily target for most adults 60+: 80–100 grams of protein per day.
